--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROJECT A:
- Course / Subject: DS312 Data Mining & Applications (DMA)
- Project Title / Topic: Socioeconomic Vulnerability & Access to Health Services in Cagayan de Oro City
- GitHub Repository: https://github.com/Troge-dev/Socioeconomic-Vulnerability-Health-Access-CDO
- What dataset did you use?:
  OpenStreetMap (OSM) spatial healthcare infrastructure (public/private hospitals,
  barangay health stations, clinics) combined with Philippine Statistics Authority
  (PSA) and Community-Based Monitoring System (CBMS) socioeconomic indicators across
  the 80 barangays of Cagayan de Oro City.
- What was the core goal or research question?:
  Evaluating geographic healthcare equity using Enhanced Two-Step Floating
  Catchment Area (E2SFCA) methods and correlating spatial impedance travel times
  with multi-dimensional socioeconomic vulnerability indices.
- What was the hardest part or biggest roadblock?:
  Handling spatial boundary effects, non-linear travel time decay, aligning
  heterogeneous coordinate reference systems (WGS84 to UTM Zone 51N), and
  documenting healthcare telemetry gaps across remote upland rural barangays.
- What final output did you create?:
  A publication-grade scientific research report, spatial choropleth maps, and
  interactive GIS accessibility visualizers.

PROJECT B:
- Course / Subject: DS312 Data Mining & Applications (DMA)
- Project Title / Topic: Diabetes 130-US Hospitals 30-Day Readmission Prediction
- GitHub Repository: https://github.com/Troge-dev/Diabetes-130-US-Hospitals-30-Day-Readmission-Prediction
- What dataset did you use?:
  UCI Machine Learning Repository Diabetes 130-US Hospitals dataset, containing
  101,766 clinical inpatient encounters across 130 hospitals (1999-2008) tracking
  50 clinical and demographic attributes (ICD-9 diagnosis codes, medication regimens,
  HbA1c tests, and admission characteristics).
- What was the core goal or research question?:
  Predicting early 30-day patient hospital readmission to support clinical decision-making
  and prevent healthcare complications under ACA Hospital Readmissions Reduction guidelines.
- What was the hardest part or biggest roadblock?:
  Severe structural missingness (e.g., 'weight' missing in >97% of records, 'payer_code'
  missing in >40%), extreme multi-class class imbalance (<30 days vs >30 days vs No readmission),
  and managing high-cardinality categorical variables with 700+ distinct ICD-9 disease codes.
- What final output did you create?:
  An end-to-end clinical data mining pipeline, regularized predictive models,
  feature importance SHAP diagnostics, and an evaluation report.

PROJECT C:
- Course / Subject: DS311 Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
- Project Title / Topic: Laboratory Activity 1 - Water Potability Data Cleaning Pipeline
- GitHub Repository: https://github.com/Troge-dev/Water-Potability-Data-Cleaning
- What dataset did you use?:
  `water_potability.csv` (3,276 water sample records across 10 aquatic physicochemical
  parameters: pH, Hardness, Solids, Chloramines, Sulfate, Conductivity, Organic Carbon,
  Trihalomethanes, Turbidity, and Potability).
- What was the core goal or research question?:
  Our foundational imputation and data quality activity in DS311. Systematically detecting,
  auditing, and cleaning sensor probe drift, selective laboratory test omissions, and
  mineral concentration outliers to evaluate drinking water safety adhering to WHO standards.
- What was the hardest part or biggest roadblock?:
  Understanding that missingness was not uniform: expensive laboratory assays (like Gas
  Chromatography for Trihalomethanes and specialized Sulfate tests) were omitted non-randomly.
  Blind mean imputation would have created fake mineral clusters and distorted the true
  physicochemical distributions.
- What final output did you create?:
  A modular data cleaning and domain imputation notebook with distribution diagnostics
  and clean exported datasets (`water_potability_cleaned.csv`).

SECTION 2: THE "DATASET-HOPPING GRAVEYARD" & LESSONS LEARNED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The DATAVIS Pre-EDA Experience:
  In earlier semesters, encountering dirty datasets caused panic because missing values
  and weird proportional columns felt like "broken code" rather than data signals.
- The Egypt Stumble:
  Hopping through confusing datasets led to finding the Egypt Population Density dataset,
  which was clean, structured, and allowed successful mapping of the Nile corridor.
- The Topic 01 Realization:
  Finding Egypt was lucky, but avoiding messy datasets is running away from data science.
  In real-world projects like Water Potability or CDO Health Access, data is never clean
  out of the box--it requires John Tukey's exploratory mindset (Resistance, Residuals,
  Re-expression, and Revelation).

SECTION 3: CLASSROOM & LAB MEMORIES (MA'AM MUGOT'S CLASS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Ma'am Chris Jallaine S. Mugot's Golden Rule of Thumb:
  "It is very common to spend the most time in the exploratory data analysis
  stage in order to have a well made output and to prevent future inconvenience."
- Core Pedagogical Anchor:
  "Univariate comes first before bivariate and multivariate! Looking at summary
  numbers alone never gives you the whole picture--you must always visualize
  the distribution."
- Anscombe's Lesson:
  Data must be seen, not just calculated. Summary statistics can conceal completely
  different realities.
