Association Rule Mining: Mathematical Invariants & Interpretation

Formal mathematical formulations for Market Basket Analysis, clinical co-prescriptions, and co-occurring diagnoses

APRIORI PRINCIPLE

"All non-empty subsets of a frequent itemset must also be frequent." If {Metformin, Insulin} is infrequent, any superset {Metformin, Insulin, Glipizide} is guaranteed infrequent!

AGRAWAL & SRIKANT (1994) · FREQUENT PATTERN PRUNING

SUPPORT: P(X ∪ Y)

Itemset Frequency Metric

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Support Formula: Support(X → Y) = count(X ∪ Y) / N. Quantifies the global prevalence of the combined itemset across the database. Prunes rare noise.

CONFIDENCE: P(Y | X)

Rule Reliability Metric

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Confidence Formula: Confidence(X → Y) = Support(X ∪ Y) / Support(X). Conditional probability that transaction contains Y given it already contains X. Flaw: Ignores baseline popularity of Y!

LIFT: P(X ∪ Y) / (P(X)P(Y))

True Interdependence Metric

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Lift Interpretation: Lift > 1: Positive correlation (X actively triggers Y). Lift = 1: Complete independence. Lift < 1: Negative correlation / mutual repulsion (X substitutes Y).

CHECKPOINT 1 · Q1

Lift vs. High Confidence Trap

Why High Confidence is Deceptive

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
DECISION ANALYSIS

The Milk & Bread Trap

Why can a rule with 90% confidence be completely uninformative or even counterproductive?

  • The Baseline Frequency Paradox: If 95% of all hospital patients receive saline hydration, the rule {Any Drug} → {Saline} will naturally have >90% confidence simply because Saline is universally common.
  • The Lift Reality Check: Lift = P(X, Y) / (P(X)P(Y)) = 0.90 / 0.95 = 0.947 < 1.0! The presence of drug X actually slightly decreases the likelihood of saline compared to random chance!
  • Exam Invariant: High confidence alone never proves affinity. A rule is only valuable if Lift > 1.0.

CHECKPOINT 1 · Q2

The Curse of Dimensionality

Distance Metrics & Empty Space

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
GEOMETRIC ANALYSIS

Dimensionality Collapse

What happens mathematically to Euclidean distance metrics as feature count p explodes?

  • Distance Convergence: In high-dimensional hyperspace ($p > 100$), the ratio between the distance to the nearest neighbor and the distance to the farthest neighbor approaches 1.0: $\lim_{p \to \infty} (dist_{max} - dist_{min}) / dist_{min} = 0$.
  • Geometric Emptiness: Volume of the space grows exponentially ($2^p$), making all data points appear uniformly distant and isolated.
  • Clustering Failure: Distance-based clustering (K-Means, KNN) breaks down entirely, demanding PCA or feature selection prior to modeling.

Stevens' Measurement Taxonomy: Nominal, Ordinal, Interval & Ratio

Understanding variable scale invariants to prevent mathematically illegal operations in machine learning

CATEGORICAL TIERS

Nominal vs. Ordinal

Qualitative Groupings & Rankings

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
CATEGORICAL SCALES

Nominal & Ordinal Logic

Qualitative attributes governed by equivalence and order.

  • Nominal (Identity): Pure categories without inherent ordering. Operations: Mode, Count, Frequency, χ² test. Mathematical operations like Mean and Standard Deviation are completely illegal! Examples: Blood Type, Marital Status, Medical Specialty.
  • Ordinal (Ranking): Ordered hierarchical categories where distance between ranks is unknown or uneven. Operations: Median, Percentiles, Rank-order correlations (Spearman ρ). Examples: Cancer Staging (Stage I to IV), Pain Scale (1 to 10).
← RETURN TO SUMMARY ↻
NUMERICAL TIERS

Interval vs. Ratio

Quantitative Distances & True Zeros

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
NUMERICAL SCALES

Interval & Ratio Logic

Quantitative measurements with continuous arithmetic properties.

  • Interval (Arbitrary Zero): Equal measurable units but arbitrary zero point (0 does not mean absolute absence). Operations: Addition, Subtraction, Mean, Variance. Ratios are invalid (20°C is NOT twice as hot as 10°C). Examples: Temperature (°C/°F), Calendar Year.
  • Ratio (Absolute Zero): Equal measurable units with a true, non-arbitrary zero point (0 means complete absence). Operations: All mathematical operations including Division and Ratios (400 mg is exactly twice 200 mg). Examples: Patient Age, Length of Stay, Blood Glucose.

Missing Data Taxonomy & Imputation Decision Engine

Auditing Rubin's mechanisms, running Little's test, and selecting statistically valid imputation strategies

RUBIN'S MISSINGNESS TAXONOMY

MCAR vs. MAR vs. MNAR

Distinguishing Mechanisms of Data Loss

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
RUBIN TAXONOMY

Missing Data Mechanisms

Formal classifications of data loss mechanisms.

  • MCAR (Completely at Random): Missingness is entirely independent of observed and unobserved data. Probability of missingness is uniform across all rows. Verified by Little's MCAR test (p > 0.05). Listwise deletion is statistically unbiased.
  • MAR (At Random): Missingness depends systematically on other observed variables, but not on the missing value itself (e.g., older patients less likely to fill online weight survey). Solved via Multiple Imputation by Chained Equations (MICE) or KNN.
  • MNAR (Not at Random): Missingness depends directly on the unobserved value itself (e.g., severely depressed patients refusing to complete depression severity questionnaire). Requires pattern-mixture modeling or explicit missing indicator flags.

IMPUTATION STRATEGY

Decision Tree for Missingness

Algorithmic protocol for treating missing values.

  • Missingness > 50%: Drop the column unless domain expertise confirms extreme clinical value (e.g. Weight variable in Diabetes 130-US Hospitals is 97% missing → drop column).
  • Symmetric Continuous (MCAR): Impute with Mean if distribution is verified Gaussian.
  • Skewed Continuous / Ordinal: Impute with Median to resist outlier distortion.
  • Categorical Nominal: Impute with Mode or establish a dedicated "Missing" / "Unknown" category.
  • Multivariate Relationships: Use IterativeImputer (MICE) or KNNImputer to preserve covariance structure across features.

Feature Transformation, Scaling & The Dummy Variable Trap

Mathematical behavior of normalization vs. standardization, and preventing singular matrix inversion failure

ALGORITHMIC SENSITIVITY

Tree-based models (Random Forest, XGBoost) are scale-invariant. Distance-based models (KNN, K-Means, SVM, PCA) and gradient models are critically sensitive to scaling!

DS312 FEATURE ENGINEERING INVARIANT

MIN-MAX SCALING: [0, 1]

Bound-Constrained Normalization

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Formula: (x - min) / (max - min). Compresses features to exact [0, 1] range. Ideal for neural networks and image data, but catastrophic if extreme outliers exist (collapses in-bounds data into tiny band).

STANDARD Z-SCORE SCALING

Gaussian Zero-Mean Standardization

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Formula: (x - μ) / σ. Centers feature at mean 0 with standard deviation 1. Preserves outlier distances; essential for linear models, SVMs, and PCA.

ROBUST SCALER: IQR-BASED

Outlier-Resistant Centering

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Formula: (x - Median) / IQR. Uses median and interquartile range; completely unaffected by extreme anomalies. Gold standard for clinical healthcare datasets with high skew.

THE DUMMY VARIABLE TRAP: k - 1

Avoiding Perfect Multicollinearity

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Linear Dependency: If a categorical feature has k categories, encoding all k dummy columns introduces perfect collinearity (∑ D_i = 1), causing singular matrix inversion failure (X^T X)^{-1}. Always drop 1 baseline column (drop_first=True)!

Parametric vs. Non-Parametric Statistical Testing Matrix

Systematic validation of LINE+M assumptions and selecting valid hypothesis tests across variable scales

PARAMETRIC ASSUMPTIONS (LINE+M)

Strict Gaussian Distribution Criteria

Linearity, Independence, Normality, Equal Var

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
PARAMETRIC CRITERIA

LINE + M Assumptions

Mandatory mathematical criteria before using parametric tests.

  • Linearity (L): Linear relationship between features and outcome variable.
  • Independence (I): Observations are independent (Durbin-Watson test ~ 2.0).
  • Normality (N): Residuals normally distributed (Shapiro-Wilk test p > 0.05).
  • Equal Variance / Homoscedasticity (E): Constant residual spread (Levene's test p > 0.05).
  • Multicollinearity (M): No extreme feature collinearity (VIF < 5.0).

HYPOTHESIS TEST SELECTION MATRIX

Parametric vs. Non-Parametric Equivalence

Statistical Tests by Scale and Groups

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
STATISTICAL MATRIX

Test Equivalence Framework

Mapping experimental designs to appropriate hypothesis tests.

  • Two Independent Groups: Two-sample t-test (Parametric) ↔ Mann-Whitney U test (Non-Parametric). Tests differences in medians when data is skewed.
  • Two Paired / Repeated Groups: Paired t-test (Parametric) ↔ Wilcoxon Signed-Rank test (Non-Parametric).
  • Three or More Groups: One-Way ANOVA (Parametric) ↔ Kruskal-Wallis H test (Non-Parametric).
  • Two Categorical Variables: Chi-Square (χ²) Test of Independence. Invariant: All expected cell frequencies must be ≥ 5.

Checkpoint 2: Preprocessing & Statistical Testing Pitfalls

Addressing real-world assumption failures: Heteroscedasticity, Welch's correction, and Fisher's exact test

CHECKPOINT 2 · Q1

Levene's Test Failure & Welch's t-test

Handling Heteroscedasticity in Data

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
INFERENTIAL ANALYSIS

Variance Violation

What must a data scientist do if Levene's test rejects equal variance (p < 0.05) when comparing two groups?

  • The Equal Variance Violation: Standard Student's t-test strictly assumes $\sigma_1^2 = \sigma_2^2$. If Levene's test yields $p < 0.05$, equal variance is refuted (heteroscedasticity).
  • The Correct Correction: Switch immediately to Welch's t-test (set `equal_var=False` in `scipy.stats.ttest_ind`), which adjusts degrees of freedom via the Welch-Satterthwaite equation.
  • Non-Parametric Alternative: If normality is also violated, execute the Mann-Whitney U rank-sum test.

CHECKPOINT 2 · Q2

The Chi-Square Cell Count Violation

Expected Frequencies & Fisher's Exact Test

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
CONTINGENCY ANALYSIS

Chi-Square Rule of 5

What happens if a 2×2 contingency table has expected cell frequencies less than 5?

  • The Approximation Breakdown: The asymptotic χ² distribution approximates the discrete multinomial distribution accurately only when all expected cell frequencies $E_{ij} \ge 5$.
  • Failure Mode: With small expected counts ($E_{ij} < 5$), χ² p-values are heavily biased towards false positives (Type I error inflation).
  • The Invariant Solution: For 2×2 tables, replace χ² with Fisher's Exact Test (`scipy.stats.fisher_exact`), which computes exact hypergeometric probabilities.

Bayesian Topic Modeling: Latent Dirichlet Allocation & Coherence

BAYESIAN DIRICHLET PRIORS

α (Doc-Topic) & β (Topic-Word)

Hyperparameter Sparsity Control

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
DIRICHLET PRIORS

Alpha & Beta Hyperparameters

Controlling document and topic distributions.

  • Alpha (α Prior): Controls document-topic sparsity θ_d. Low α (< 0.1) forces documents to consist of only 1 or 2 dominant topics. High α produces uniform multi-topic mixtures.
  • Beta (β / η Prior): Controls topic-word sparsity φ_k. Low β forces topics to be characterized by a concise, sharp set of specialized keywords. High β blurs vocabulary across topics.

MODEL EVALUATION: C_v VS. PERPLEXITY

Why Coherence Beats Perplexity

Human Interpretability vs. Likelihood

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
EVALUATION METRICS

Coherence Score (C_v)

Resolving the statistical paradox in topic modeling.

  • Perplexity Paradox: Statistically lower perplexity on held-out text often yields incoherent, uninterpretable "word salad" to human experts.
  • Semantic Coherence (C_v): Measures sliding-window NPMI and cosine similarity among top-N topic words (0 to 1). Scores > 0.55 indicate tight semantic themes.
  • Optimal K Selection: Iterate K from 2 to 20 topics; choose the peak or elbow of the C_v curve to maximize clinical interpretability.
