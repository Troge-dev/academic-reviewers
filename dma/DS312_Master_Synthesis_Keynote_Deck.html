MODULE 3 · MARKET BASKET DISCOVERY

Association Rules: Mathematical Invariants & Apriori Pruning

Mathematical formulations governing affinity discovery: Support, Confidence, Lift, and Anti-Monotonicity

THE APRIORI PRINCIPLE

Anti-Monotonicity of Support

"All non-empty subsets of a frequent itemset must also be frequent."

The Contrapositive Invariant: If itemset {A, B} is INFREQUENT (fails min_support), then ANY superset containing them — such as {A, B, C} or {A, B, C, D} — is guaranteed INFREQUENT and can be immediately pruned without scanning transactions!
SEARCH SPACE COMPRESSION: Exponential O(2ᵈ) reduced to manageable polynomial time.
FORMULA 1 · CO-OCCURRENCE FREQUENCY

Support: P(X ∪ Y)

$$\text{Support}(X \implies Y) = \frac{\sigma(X \cup Y)}{N} = P(X \cap Y)$$

Fraction of total transactions containing both antecedent X and consequent Y.

FLIP FOR CLINICAL TRAP ↻

Support Threshold Sensitivity

Setting min_support too high misses rare, lethal adverse drug reactions (e.g., Anaphylaxis co-prescriptions occurring in only 0.05% of patients). Setting it too low causes combinatorial explosion.

RETURN ↻
FORMULA 2 · CONDITIONAL CERTAINTY

Confidence: P(Y | X)

$$\text{Confidence}(X \implies Y) = \frac{\sigma(X \cup Y)}{\sigma(X)} = P(Y | X)$$

Conditional probability that transaction contains Y given it already contains X.

FLIP FOR CONFIDENCE FLAW ↻

The Fatal Flaw of Confidence

Confidence completely ignores the baseline popularity of Y! If 90% of all shoppers buy Milk regardless of what else is in their cart, ANY item X will show high confidence to Milk, even if X actually depresses Milk purchases!

RETURN ↻
FORMULA 3 · INDEPENDENCE BENCHMARK

Lift: P(X ∪ Y) / [P(X) · P(Y)]

$$\text{Lift}(X \implies Y) = \frac{P(X \cap Y)}{P(X) \cdot P(Y)}$$

Ratio of observed joint support to expected support under statistical independence.

FLIP FOR 3-WAY INTERPRETATION ↻

The 3-Way Lift Invariant

  • Lift > 1: Positive affinity! Buying X increases probability of buying Y.
  • Lift = 1: Total statistical independence. X and Y co-occur purely by chance.
  • Lift < 1: Negative affinity / substitute goods! Buying X repels buying Y.
RETURN ↻
MODULE 4 · MEASUREMENT FOUNDATIONS

Stevens' Measurement Taxonomy: Nominal, Ordinal, Interval, Ratio

S.S. Stevens (1946): The mathematical scale dictates permissible statistical operations and models

QUALITATIVE UNORDERED

Nominal Scale

Labels, categories, and identifiers. Equality (=, ≠) is the only mathematical relation.

FLIP FOR PERMISSIBLE MATH & RISKS ↻

Nominal Analytical Invariants

  • Permissible Statistics: Mode, Frequency Counts, Chi-Square (χ²) test of independence.
  • Forbidden Math: Mean, Median, Variance, Standard Deviation.
  • Encoding Trap: Label-encoding Nominal data as integers (e.g. Red=1, Blue=2) tricks linear regression into hallucinating artificial ordinal hierarchy!
RETURN ↻
QUALITATIVE ORDERED

Ordinal Scale

Meaningful order and ranks, but non-uniform distance between adjacent intervals (<, >).

FLIP FOR PERMISSIBLE MATH & RISKS ↻

Ordinal Analytical Invariants

  • Permissible Statistics: Median, Percentiles, Interquartile Range (IQR), Spearman's Rho.
  • Examples: Cancer Stages (Stage I, II, III, IV), Likert Satisfaction scales (1–5).
  • Violation: The difference between Stage I and II is NOT biologically equivalent to Stage III and IV; calculating means is mathematically invalid.
RETURN ↻
QUANTITATIVE ARBITRARY ZERO

Interval Scale

Uniform differences between values (+, -), but lacks a true physical zero point.

FLIP FOR PERMISSIBLE MATH & RISKS ↻

Interval Analytical Invariants

  • Permissible Statistics: Mean, Standard Deviation, Variance, Pearson's r, t-test, ANOVA.
  • Examples: Celsius temperature, Fahrenheit temperature, Calendar dates, IQ scores.
  • Ratio Fallacy: 20°C is NOT "twice as hot" as 10°C, because 0°C does not signify the absolute absence of thermal kinetic energy.
RETURN ↻
QUANTITATIVE ABSOLUTE ZERO

Ratio Scale

Uniform intervals plus an absolute, non-arbitrary true physical zero (*, /, +, -).

FLIP FOR PERMISSIBLE MATH & RISKS ↻

Ratio Analytical Invariants

  • Permissible Statistics: Geometric Mean, Coefficient of Variation, all mathematical ops.
  • Examples: Kelvin temperature, Blood Glucose (mg/dL), Hospital Length of Stay (days).
  • True Multiplicative Ratios: A hospital stay of 10 days is genuinely twice as long as 5 days; 0 days indicates zero hospital occupancy.
RETURN ↻
MODULE 4 · DATA HYGIENE ENGINE

Missing Data Diagnostics: Rubin's Taxonomy & Little's Test

Donald Rubin (1976): Classifying missingness mechanisms to prevent fatal sample selection bias

MECHANISM 1 · BENIGN

MCAR

P(M | Yₒ, Yₘ) = P(M)

Missing Completely at Random. Missingness is entirely independent of all observed and unobserved data.

FLIP FOR DIAGNOSTIC & REMEDY ↻

MCAR Technical Profile

  • Test: Little's Multivariate MCAR Test. If p > 0.05, retain null hypothesis of MCAR.
  • Real Example: A test tube slips and shatters on the laboratory floor purely at random.
  • Safe Remedy: Listwise deletion (complete case analysis) or simple mean/median imputation leaves estimators unbiased.
RETURN ↻
MECHANISM 2 · CONDITIONAL

MAR

P(M | Yₒ, Yₘ) = P(M | Yₒ)

Missing at Random. Missingness depends systematically on observed covariates, but NOT on the missing values themselves.

FLIP FOR DIAGNOSTIC & REMEDY ↻

MAR Technical Profile

  • Test: Bivariate t-tests / Chi-square comparing missing vs observed cohorts on other features.
  • Real Example: Older patients (observed) skip an optional mobile app depression questionnaire.
  • Remedy: MICE (Chained Equations), KNN Imputation, or MissForest. Listwise deletion causes severe bias!
RETURN ↻
MECHANISM 3 · NON-IGNORABLE

MNAR

P(M | Yₒ, Yₘ) ≠ P(M | Yₒ)

Missing Not at Random. Missingness is directly determined by the unobserved value itself.

FLIP FOR DIAGNOSTIC & REMEDY ↻

MNAR Technical Profile

  • Test: Cannot be verified empirically from observed data alone; requires sensitivity modeling.
  • Real Example: Patients with severe active clinical depression refuse to complete depression surveys.
  • Remedy: Pattern-mixture models, Heckman two-step selection models, or explicit binary missingness indicator flags.
RETURN ↻
MODULE 4 · FEATURE ENGINEERING

Outlier Hygiene & Feature Scaling Showdown

1.5 × IQR Tukey Fences & Winsorization vs. Min-Max, Z-Score, and Robust Scaling

ANOMALY ENGINE

1.5 × IQR Fences & Winsorization

John Tukey's non-parametric fences: Lower = Q₁ - 1.5·IQR; Upper = Q₃ + 1.5·IQR.

Why Winsorize instead of Trimming? Trimming discards rows, reducing sample size N and distorting standard errors. Winsorization caps extreme values at the 5th and 95th percentiles, preserving total N!
FLIP FOR WORKED CODE & MATH ↻

Winsorization Implementation Rule

# Preserving N while mitigating leverage from scipy.stats import mstats # Cap bottom 5% and top 5% df['glucose_winsor'] = mstats.winsorize(df['glucose'], limits=[0.05, 0.05]) # Tukey 1.5*IQR Fences IQR = df['glucose'].quantile(0.75) - df['glucose'].quantile(0.25)
RETURN ↻

Min-Max vs. Z-Score vs. Robust

Bringing disparate feature domains onto a uniform mathematical scale without distortion.

Always split into Train and Test splits FIRST. Fit the scaler exclusively on X_train, then use transform() on both X_train and X_test. Fitting on the entire dataset leaks future test distribution parameters into training!

MODULE 4 · INFERENTIAL STATISTICS

LINE+M Parametric Gate & The Hypothesis Testing Arena

Before interpreting p-values or regression coefficients, verify structural assumptions

THE LINE+M GATEWAY

Parametric OLS Assumptions

L — Linearity: Target y is a linear combination of features. (Residuals vs Fitted plot shows horizontal band).
I — Independence: Observations are uncorrelated. (Durbin-Watson d ≈ 2.0; no autocorrelation).
N — Normality: Residuals ε ∼ N(0, σ²). (Shapiro-Wilk test or Q-Q plot).
E — Equal Variance: Homoscedasticity. Constant error variance across predictions. (Breusch-Pagan p > 0.05).
M — Multicollinearity: Predictors must not be collinear. (Variance Inflation Factor VIF < 5; >10 is lethal).
Violation Remedy: Non-normality → Log/Box-Cox transform or Mann-Whitney; Heteroscedasticity → WLS or White's robust SEs.
TWO INDEPENDENT GROUPS

Two-Sample Welch's t-test vs. Mann-Whitney U

Comparing means of continuous response across 2 groups (e.g., Readmitted vs Not Readmitted on Glucose).

FLIP FOR DECISION RULE ↻

Two Groups Decision Rule

Always prefer Welch's t-test over Student's t-test because it does not assume equal variances. If residuals strongly violate normality, switch to non-parametric Mann-Whitney U test (evaluates rank sums).

RETURN ↻
THREE OR MORE GROUPS

One-Way ANOVA vs. Kruskal-Wallis H

Comparing means across 3+ categories (e.g., Length of Stay across Cardiology, Oncology, and ICU).

FLIP FOR DECISION RULE ↻

3+ Groups Decision Rule

ANOVA evaluates between-group variance vs within-group variance (F-statistic). If ANOVA is significant (p < 0.05), follow with Tukey's HSD post-hoc test to identify which specific pairs differ. If non-normal, use Kruskal-Wallis.

RETURN ↻
CATEGORICAL × CATEGORICAL

Pearson's χ² Test of Independence

Contingency table evaluating association between discrete variables (e.g. Insulin Status × Readmission).

FLIP FOR DECISION RULE ↻

Chi-Square Invariants

$$\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E} \quad \text{with } df = (r-1)(c-1)$$. Invariant: Expected frequencies in all contingency cells must exceed 5. If any cell E < 5, Cochran's rule is violated; use Fisher's Exact Test instead!

RETURN ↻

AI ⊃ ML ⊃ DL ⊃ LLM: Distinguishing learning paradigms by supervision signal and objective function

Supervised Learning

Pairs (xᵢ, yᵢ) provided. Algorithm learns mapping f: X → Y via Empirical Risk Minimization.

FLIP FOR OBJECTIVE & LOSS ↻

Supervised Empirical Objective

$$\min_w \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \mathcal{L}(y_i, f(x_i; w)) + \lambda \Omega(w)$$. Penalizes misclassifications and prediction errors against known ground truth labels.

Unsupervised Learning

Only {xᵢ} provided; no labels. Learns probability density P(X), clusters, or low-dimensional manifolds.

FLIP FOR OBJECTIVE & LOSS ↻

Navigating the fundamental algorithmic trade-off: Ockham's simplicity vs. non-linear capacity

THE PARETO INVARIANT

No Algorithm Dominates Both

As model capacity increases to capture high-order feature interactions, direct parameter inspectability decays toward zero.

Regulatory & Clinical Reality: Under GDPR Article 22 ("Right to Explanation") and FDA Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) requirements, a 94% accurate "black-box" neural net may be legally banned if a physician cannot audit why a patient was denied ICU admission.

White-Box Profile

Strength: Instant legal, clinical, and scientific defensibility. Limitation: High inductive bias; cannot capture non-linear or non-additive boundaries without manual feature interactions.

MODULE 6 · GENERALIZATION HYGIENE

The Parable of Student A vs. B & 7 Engineering Deployment Sins

Why models with 99% training accuracy fail catastrophically in clinical production

THE GENERALIZATION PARABLE

Student A vs. Student B

Student A (The Overfitter): Memorizes all 500 practice exam answers by rote memory. Achieves 100% on homework practice. When given the final exam with rephrased questions, scores 32%!
Student B (The Generalizer): Learns underlying mathematical theorems. Scores 86% on practice homework. On the unseen final exam, achieves 85%!
THE ML LAW: Minimize $\mathbb{E}_{\text{test}}[\mathcal{L}(y, f(x))]$, NOT $\mathbb{E}_{\text{train}}[\mathcal{L}(y, f(x))]$!
ENGINEERING AUTOPSY

The 7 Deadly Sins of Machine Learning

The structural failure modes that ruin production models:

  1. Target Data Leakage: Including future proxy variables in training features.
  2. Covariate Shift & Concept Drift: Test distribution differs from training environment.
  3. Train-Test Contamination: Fitting scalers/encoders before splitting.
  4. Survivorship & Selection Bias: Training only on surviving cohorts.
  5. Feedback Loops: Model predictions influence future training data collection.
  6. Cold-Start Failure: System cannot score novel users/patients.
  7. Silent Calibration Degradation: Accuracy holds, but predicted probabilities rot.
FLIP FOR MITIGATION PROTOCOL ↻

Production Safeguard Protocols

  • Leakage Audit: Any feature with correlation > 0.90 to target must be investigated for causality.
  • Temporal Split: Always validate on chronologically future time windows, never random K-Fold on time-series.
  • Continuous Monitoring: Track Population Stability Index (PSI) and Brier calibration score weekly in production.

EXAMINATION QUESTION 1

"Why is an 89% accuracy model an operational failure on Diabetes 130-Hospitals?"

The Defense: Because the dataset exhibits severe class imbalance (11.16% readmissions). A zero-intelligence dummy classifier predicting the majority class achieves 88.84% accuracy while having a Recall of exactly 0.0%. In clinical medicine, the cost of a False Negative is fatal, rendering accuracy useless.
EXAMINATION QUESTION 2

"If Little's MCAR test yields p = 0.001, why is listwise deletion strictly forbidden?"

The Defense: p < 0.05 rejects the null hypothesis of MCAR, confirming the data is Missing at Random (MAR) or MNAR. Dropping rows with missing values systematically strips out non-random patient sub-cohorts, producing severe parameter estimation bias. Imputation (MICE, MissForest) is mandatory.
EXAMINATION QUESTION 3

"Why must lemmatization be preferred over stemming in clinical NLP pipelines?"

The Defense: Stemming applies crude heuristic string chopping (e.g. Porter), reducing distinct medical words to non-existent root stems (e.g. "coronary" vs "coroner" → "coron"). Lemmatization uses morphological vocabularies and POS tags, preserving diagnostic validity.
EXAMINATION QUESTION 4

"Why does one-hot encoding a k-category nominal feature require dropping 1 column?"

The Defense: Retaining all k dummy columns creates perfect collinearity with the regression intercept vector (∑ dᵢ = 1). This causes the feature matrix (XᵀX) to be singular and non-invertible (the Dummy Variable Trap), resulting in undefined regression coefficients and infinite variance.
