The Four Machine Learning Paradigms

Categorized by supervision signal (Ground Truth vs. Unlabeled) and environment interaction (Passive vs. Active).

2×2 MATRIX
PARADIGM 01 · FULL SUPERVISION Click to Flip ⟳

Supervised Learning

Every training instance consists of a feature vector $X$ paired with a ground-truth label $y$. The algorithm acts as a student learning from a teacher with an answer key, minimizing empirical risk.

$\mathcal{D} = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^N \quad \text{Objective: } \hat{y} = f(x) \approx y$
Classification Regression Labeled Datasets
SUPERVISED MECHANICS Click to Flip ⟳

The Ground-Truth Dependency

Supervised models cannot learn patterns that were never labeled by human annotators.

  • Bottleneck: Annotation costs (e.g., radiologist lesion contours).
  • Vulnerability: Label noise and human inter-rater variability.
  • Strength: Quantifiable error metric directly tied to business targets.
PARADIGM 02 · ZERO SUPERVISION Click to Flip ⟳

Unsupervised Learning

Given only unlabeled feature vectors $X$ without target responses $y$. The algorithm explores latent geometry, probability density, and item co-occurrences without human assistance.

$\mathcal{D} = \{x_i\}_{i=1}^N \quad \text{Objective: } P(x) \text{ or } \text{Cluster}(x)$
K-Means Clustering Apriori Rules PCA Reduction
UNSUPERVISED MECHANICS Click to Flip ⟳

Subjectivity & Validation Limits

Because there is no ground-truth answer key, unsupervised learning has no objective "accuracy" score:

  • Heuristics: Relies on distance metrics (Euclidean, Manhattan, Cosine).
  • Validation: Silhouette Score, Davies-Bouldin, or business utility.
  • Risk: Finding spurious patterns that reflect data artifacts rather than reality.
PARADIGM 03 · HYBRID SUPERVISION Click to Flip ⟳

Semi-Supervised Learning

Exploits extreme real-world data asymmetry: a tiny set of expensive labeled records $L$ combined with a massive sea of cheap unlabeled data $U$, using pseudo-labeling and consistency regularization.

$\mathcal{D} = \{L \cup U\} \quad \text{where } |U| \gg |L|$
Pseudo-Labeling Consistency Loss FixMatch
SEMI-SUPERVISED MECHANICS Click to Flip ⟳

Confidence Thresholding & Danger

Semi-supervised pipelines train a preliminary model on $L$, predict probabilities on $U$, and ingest predictions with confidence $\tau \ge 0.95$ as pseudo-labels.

  • Benefit: Cuts manual annotation budgets by up to 90%.
  • Fatal Danger: Confirmation Bias—misclassified edge cases corrupt subsequent training iterations.
PARADIGM 04 · ACTIVE INTERACTION Click to Flip ⟳

Reinforcement Learning

No static dataset. An autonomous Agent interacts with a dynamic Environment, executing Actions $a_t$ based on State $s_t$, receiving scalar Rewards $r_t$, and optimizing a long-term Policy $\pi(a|s)$.

$\max_\pi \mathbb{E}\left[ \sum_{t=0}^\infty \gamma^t r_t \right] \quad (\text{Bellman MDP})$
Markov Decision Process Q-Learning Policy Gradients
RL MECHANICS Click to Flip ⟳

Credit Assignment & Exploration

Unlike supervised learning where every input receives an immediate target, RL deals with delayed rewards:

  • Credit Assignment: Which specific move in a 60-move chess game led to the win?
  • Exploration vs. Exploitation: Trying new moves vs. cashing in on known strategies ($\epsilon$-greedy).
MATHEMATICAL MECHANICS

Supervised Learning: Empirical Risk & Loss

How models learn: Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM) and loss surface optimization.

ERM FRAMEWORK
CORE THEOREM Click to Flip ⟳

Empirical Risk Minimization (ERM)

In supervised learning, the true joint distribution $P(X, Y)$ is unknown. We approximate true risk by minimizing empirical loss over our finite training set $\mathcal{D}$:

$\min_{\theta} \mathcal{R}_{\text{emp}}(\theta) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \mathcal{L}\big(y_i, f(x_i; \theta)\big) + \lambda \Omega(\theta)$

$\mathcal{L}(y, \hat{y})$: The instantaneous penalty function quantifying disagreement between prediction and ground truth.

$\lambda \Omega(\theta)$: The regularization penalty ($L_1$ Lasso or $L_2$ Ridge) that constrains model complexity to prevent overfitting.

Optimization: Gradient Descent iteratively updates parameter weights: $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \eta \nabla_\theta \mathcal{R}_{\text{emp}}$.

Loss Landscape Gradient Descent Regularization
GENERALIZATION GAP Click to Flip ⟳

Empirical Risk vs. True Risk

The primary danger in ERM is that a model can achieve $\mathcal{R}_{\text{emp}} = 0$ simply by memorizing the training noise, while suffering massive true generalization error on unseen test data.

  • Generalization Gap: $\Delta = |\mathcal{R}_{\text{true}}(\theta) - \mathcal{R}_{\text{emp}}(\theta)|$.
  • Vapnik-Chervonenkis (VC) Dimension: Measures the capacity/flexibility of the hypothesis space. Higher capacity requires strictly more data to guarantee generalization.
DISCRETE LOSS Click to Flip ⟳

Binary Cross-Entropy Loss

For classification tasks where $y \in \{0, 1\}$ and predicted probability $\hat{y} \in [0, 1]$:

$\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}} = -\big[y \ln(\hat{y}) + (1 - y) \ln(1 - \hat{y})\big]$

Imposes an asymptotic penalty approaching infinity if the model predicts $\hat{y} \approx 0$ when the true class is $y = 1$.

MULTICLASS EXTENSION Click to Flip ⟳

Categorical Cross-Entropy

Generalized across $K$ mutually exclusive categories via the Softmax activation:

$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} = -\sum_{k=1}^K y_k \ln\left(\frac{e^{z_k}}{\sum_{j} e^{z_j}}\right)$

Directly equivalent to maximizing the log-likelihood of the observed data under a Multinomial distribution.

CONTINUOUS LOSS Click to Flip ⟳

Mean Squared Error (MSE)

For continuous regression targets where $y, \hat{y} \in \mathbb{R}$:

$\text{MSE} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2$

Squares the residuals, heavily penalizing large estimation blunders while remaining mathematically smooth and differentiable.

ROBUST ALTERNATIVE Click to Flip ⟳

Mean Absolute Error & Huber Loss

Because MSE squares residuals, it is violently distorted by outliers. In noisy industrial environments, we switch to:

$\text{MAE} = \frac{1}{N} \sum |y_i - \hat{y}_i| \quad (\text{Laplace Prior})$

Huber Loss: Combines quadratic MSE for small residuals with linear MAE for errors exceeding threshold $\delta$.

SUB-TYPE A · DISCRETE TARGET Click to Flip ⟳

Classification

Maps feature vectors to categorical labels $y \in \{C_1, C_2, \dots, C_k\}$. The objective is to construct optimal decision boundaries that separate classes in feature space.

Output Space: Discrete Classes / Probabilities
Boundary: Sigmoid $\sigma(w^T x) = 0.5$ or Tree Partitions
Evaluation: Precision, Recall, F1, ROC-AUC
  • Binary: Fraud vs. Legitimate; Sepsis vs. Non-Sepsis.
  • Multiclass: Handwritten Digits (0–9); Lesion Types.
  • Models: Logistic Regression, Decision Trees, SVM, Naive Bayes.
Decision Boundary Confusion Matrix Threshold Tuning
DECISION THRESHOLDS Click to Flip ⟳

The Probability Threshold Illusion

Classification models do not output classes; they output probabilities $P(Y=1|X)$. The assignment to a discrete class depends entirely on the decision threshold $\tau \in [0, 1]$.

  • Standard $\tau = 0.5$: Catastrophic in medical screening or fraud.
  • Cost-Sensitive Optimization: Lowering $\tau \rightarrow 0.15$ in sepsis alerting to maximize Recall ($F_2$-Score), accepting false positives to prevent patient mortality.
SUB-TYPE B · CONTINUOUS TARGET Click to Flip ⟳

Regression

Models the mathematical relationship between predictors and an infinite quantitative real-valued response $y \in \mathbb{R}$. Fits continuous trend hyperplanes or manifolds.

Output Space: Real Numbers $\mathbb{R}$ (Unbounded or $[0, \infty)$)
Surface: $\hat{y} = \beta_0 + \sum \beta_j x_j$
Evaluation: $R^2$, RMSE, MAE, MAPE
  • Forecasting: Stock valuation, retail customer lifetime value.
  • Physical Modeling: House prices, blood pressure, temperature.
  • Models: OLS, Ridge, Lasso, ElasticNet, SVR, Random Forest.
Residual Surface Homoscedasticity Multicollinearity
PARAMETRIC ASSUMPTIONS Click to Flip ⟳

The LINE+M Foundation

Linear regression models are only valid if their residuals satisfy the Gauss-Markov theorem:

  • L (Linearity): Linear relationship between $X$ and $Y$.
  • I (Independence): Durbin-Watson statistic $\approx 2.0$.
  • N (Normality): Residuals normally distributed $\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$.
  • E (Equal Variance): Homoscedasticity (no fan-out).
  • M (No Multicollinearity): Variance Inflation Factor $\text{VIF} < 5$.
UNSUPERVISED SUB-TYPES

Unsupervised Learning: Latent Geometry

Extracting natural clusters, transactional association rules, and orthogonal dimensional projections.

TRI-MODAL
TECHNIQUE 01 · TOPOLOGICAL PARTITIONING Click to Flip ⟳

Clustering: K-Means vs. Hierarchical vs. DBSCAN

Voronoi Cells Dendrograms Density Noise Filter
K-Means (Centroid)

Minimizes Within-Cluster Sum of Squares (WCSS). Assumes spherical, equal-sized convex clusters. Highly sensitive to outliers.

Hierarchical (Linkage)

Builds bottom-up agglomerative dendrograms. Ward's linkage minimizes variance. No need to pre-specify $K$ beforehand.

DBSCAN (Density)

Discovers arbitrary non-convex shapes based on $\epsilon$-neighborhood and MinPts. Automatically isolates outliers as noise (-1).

MATHEMATICAL VALIDATION Click to Flip ⟳

How Do We Validate Clusters Without Ground Truth?

Without an answer key $y$, clustering performance must be validated mathematically via internal geometric cohesion and separation:

$\text{Silhouette Score: } s(i) = \frac{b(i) - a(i)}{\max(a(i), b(i))} \in [-1, +1]$

Where $a(i)$ is mean intra-cluster distance and $b(i)$ is mean nearest-cluster distance. Scores near $+1.0$ indicate pristine, compact, highly isolated cluster topology.

TECHNIQUE 02 · CO-OCCURRENCE Click to Flip ⟳

Association Rule Mining

Discovers transactional dependencies formatted as "If $X \rightarrow$ Then $Y$". Governed by the Apriori Principle (all subsets of a frequent itemset must be frequent).

$\text{Lift}(X \rightarrow Y) = \frac{\text{Support}(X \cup Y)}{\text{Support}(X) \times \text{Support}(Y)}$

$\text{Lift} > 1.0$ proves statistical affinity beyond independent random chance.

INDUSTRIAL APPLICATION Click to Flip ⟳

Market Basket & Cross-Selling

E-commerce recommendation engines and physical store layouts optimize shelf adjacency based on high-lift itemsets (e.g. Diapers $\rightarrow$ Beer at 6 PM).

TECHNIQUE 03 · PROJECTION Click to Flip ⟳

Dimensionality Reduction (PCA)

Projects high-dimensional feature spaces onto lower-dimensional orthogonal principal components while maximizing preserved data variance and defeating the Curse of Dimensionality.

$\Sigma v_i = \lambda_i v_i \quad (\text{Covariance Eigendecomposition})$

Eigenvectors $v_i$ define component directions; eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ quantify explained variance.

THE CURSE OF DIMENSIONALITY Click to Flip ⟳

Why Reduction is Mandatory

As feature dimensions $p \rightarrow \infty$, data space volume expands exponentially, observations become mutually equidistant, and distance metrics collapse. PCA restores density by compressing correlated axes into orthogonal bases.

Weak data augmentation (horizontal flips, gentle crops) is applied to ensure the scored representation reflects true invariant semantics.

PHASE 03 Flip ⟳

Confidence Gate ($\tau$)

Only predictions with statistical confidence exceeding extreme threshold $\tau \ge 0.95$ are converted into official pseudo-labels.

$\mathbb{I}\big(\max(\hat{p}) \ge \tau\big)$
CONSISTENCY LOSS

FixMatch: Enforces cross-entropy loss against a strongly augmented version of the identical image using the pseudo-label as ground truth.

PHASE 04 Flip ⟳

Confirmation Bias

If the model makes an overconfident mistake (e.g. 98% confident on a malignant tumor mislabeled as benign), it ingests its own error into $L$.

Error Cascading Loop
DEFENSE SAFEGUARD

Safeguards include conservative thresholds ($\tau = 0.98$), temperature scaling, and curriculum pseudo-label loss weighting $\lambda_u L_u$.

OPTIMALITY EQUATION Click to Flip ⟳

The Bellman Equation

The expected value of taking action $a$ in state $s$ equals the immediate reward plus discounted future value:

$V^*(s) = \max_a \left[ R(s, a) + \gamma \sum_{s'} P(s'|s, a) V^*(s') \right]$

Discount factor $\gamma \in [0, 1)$ balances immediate gratification against strategic long-term survival.

REINFORCEMENT ALGORITHMS Click to Flip ⟳

Value-Based vs. Policy-Based

  • Q-Learning / DQN: Learns the expected utility $Q(s, a)$ of taking action $a$ in state $s$.
  • Policy Gradients (PPO): Parameterizes policy $\pi_\theta(a|s)$ directly, adjusting weights via gradient ascent on expected return.
FUNDAMENTAL DILEMMA Click to Flip ⟳

Exploration vs. Exploitation

Should the agent exploit its current knowledge to maximize reward, or explore uncharted states to discover superior long-term strategies?

$\epsilon\text{-Greedy: Action } a = \begin{cases} \text{Random Action} & \text{prob } \epsilon \\ \text{argmax}_a Q(s, a) & \text{prob } 1-\epsilon \end{cases}$
EXPLORATION COLLAPSE Click to Flip ⟳

Suboptimal Trapping

If $\epsilon$ decays to zero too quickly, the agent exploits a mediocre local optimum and never discovers the globally optimal policy. E.g., an autonomous car always taking a slow backroad because it never explored the highway.

POST-HOC EXPLAINABILITY LIMITS Click to Flip ⟳

SHAP and LIME Are Approximations

Techniques like SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) and LIME approximate the black-box model locally with a linear surrogate. They do not turn a black box into a glass box:

  • Adversarial Vulnerability: SHAP values can be fooled by adversarial perturbations.
  • Fidelity Tradeoff: The local explanation may be 90% accurate to the black box, but that 10% delta can hide illegal demographic bias.
GLASS-BOX INTRINSICS Click to Flip ⟳

Why Simple Models Are Explainable

Linear and logistic regression have separable, additive parameters. Each coefficient $\beta_j$ represents the exact change in output for a unit increase in feature $x_j$, holding all other variables constant.

$\frac{\partial \hat{y}}{\partial x_j} = \beta_j \quad (\text{Constant Marginal Impact})$
GLASS-BOX VULNERABILITY Click to Flip ⟳

Underfitting Non-Linear Realities

The linearity assumption is almost universally false in complex real-world biology and economics. Forcing a linear hyperplane onto complex interactive manifolds introduces high bias and lowers predictive utility.

BLACK-BOX MECHANICS Click to Flip ⟳

Why Complex Models Become Opaque

Ensembles combine hundreds of randomized trees; deep networks stack millions of non-linear matrix multiplications $f(x) = \sigma(W_L \dots \sigma(W_1 x))$. A single input feature influences thousands of pathways simultaneously.

$\frac{\partial \hat{y}}{\partial x_j} = g(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_p) \quad (\text{Non-separable})$
REGULATORY REJECTION Click to Flip ⟳

Statutory Ineligibility

In regulated banking and healthcare, an unexplainable model cannot be deployed even if it boasts 99.9% accuracy, because regulatory bodies mandate auditable adverse action notices.

Bias-Variance Tradeoff
MODEL ORAL DEFENSE

"Generalization error decomposes into $\text{MSE} = \text{Bias}^2(\hat{f}) + \text{Var}(\hat{f}) + \sigma^2$. Complex models suffer from high variance. Lasso ($L_1: \lambda \sum |\beta_j|$) introduces diamond-shaped geometric penalty boundaries that intersect parameter axes at sharp vertices. This forces non-essential weights to exactly zero, increasing bias slightly in exchange for a massive reduction in variance and executing automated feature selection."
