Retrospective Discovery

The systematic process of extracting novel patterns from stored repositories.

MACHINE LEARNING AXIOMS

Predictive Generalization

Algorithms that improve performance metrics with experience.

Supervised vs. Unsupervised

Learning governed by presence or absence of target variables.

Given training dataset D = {(x_1, y_1), (x_2, y_2), ..., (x_n, y_n)}, find function f(x) that minimizes expected risk: E[L(y, f(x))].

Supervised Tasks: Classification vs. Regression

Distinguishing categorical class boundary partitioning from continuous numerical curve approximation

DISCRETE TARGET: y ∈ {C_1, ..., C_k}

Supervised Classification

Predicting Qualitative Class Categories

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
CLASSIFICATION ANALYSIS

Qualitative Boundary Slicing

Categorizing observations into discrete categorical buckets.

  • Target Output: Categorical class label (binary, multi-class, or multi-label). E.g. Spam / Not Spam, Sepsis / Healthy, Credit Default (Yes/No).
  • Mathematical Decision Boundary: Partitions feature space into regions separated by decision hyperplanes or non-linear manifolds.
  • Core Algorithms: Logistic Regression (sigmoid probability), Decision Trees, Random Forests, Support Vector Machines (max-margin hyperplanes), Naive Bayes, Neural Networks.
  • Primary Objective: Maximize class separation and minimize misclassification penalty.

CONTINUOUS TARGET: y ∈ R

Supervised Regression

Predicting Quantitative Numerical Responses

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
REGRESSION ANALYSIS

Continuous Function Approximation

Estimating exact numerical quantities along continuous scales.

  • Target Output: Real-valued continuous number with meaningful arithmetic properties. E.g. Housing prices (₱4.5M), Patient Length of Stay (days), Blood Glucose (mg/dL).
  • Mathematical Curve Fitting: Estimates conditional expectation E[Y | X = x] by minimizing squared residual errors ∑ (y_i - ŷ_i)^2.
  • Core Algorithms: Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) Linear Regression, Ridge/Lasso, Polynomial Regression, Decision Tree Regressors, Gradient Boosting, Neural Networks.
  • Primary Objective: Minimize continuous distance between predictions and actual measurements.

UNSUPERVISED PATTERNS

Clustering & Rules

Organizing observations and frequent item affinities.

  • K-Means Clustering: Partitions data into K distinct Voronoi cells by iteratively updating cluster centroids to minimize within-cluster sum of squares (WCSS).
  • Hierarchical Clustering: Builds a nested dendrogram of clusters using agglomerative bottom-up linkage (Ward, Complete, Average).
  • DBSCAN: Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise. Discovers arbitrarily shaped clusters and isolates anomalous noise points.
  • Association Rules (Apriori): Identifies co-occurrence affinities in transaction records (Bread → Milk) evaluated via Support, Confidence, and Lift.

MANIFOLD COMPRESSION

Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

Orthogonal Variance Projection

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
DIMENSIONALITY REDUCTION

PCA Mechanics

Compressing feature dimensions while retaining maximum informational variance.

  • Curse of Dimensionality Relief: High-dimensional datasets (p = 500) suffer from geometric emptiness and collinearity. PCA compresses them into k ≪ p components.
  • Eigenvector Decomposition: Finds orthogonal axes of maximum variance via covariance matrix eigendecomposition or Singular Value Decomposition (SVD).
  • Data Visualization: Projects 50+ clinical or financial features onto 2D/3D scatter plots for human perceptual inspection.
  • Noise Reduction: Discards low-eigenvalue components containing predominantly white measurement noise.

No static dataset exists. The agent actively generates its own experience through interaction, optimizing cumulative discounted return: G_t = ∑ γ^k r_{t+k+1}.

The Accuracy–Interpretability Frontier: Black Boxes vs. Glass Boxes

Balancing statistical predictive power against human comprehensibility, clinical safety, and regulatory compliance

HIGH ACCURACY / OPAQUE

Complex Black-Box Models

Deep Neural Nets, Ensembles & Kernel SVM

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
BLACK-BOX ARCHITECTURES

High Accuracy / Low Transparency

Non-linear universal approximators with impenetrable internal mechanics.

  • Deep Neural Networks: Billions of non-linear parameter weights across dozens of layers. Captures complex manifolds, but internal reasoning is inscrutable.
  • Random Forests & Gradient Boosted Trees: Averages predictions across 500+ deep trees. Accurately models interactions, but impossible to audit via simple heuristics.
  • Kernel Support Vector Machines: Projects features into infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces via RBF kernels; decision boundary cannot be written as a simple equation.
  • Failure Liability: Vulnerable to hidden biases, shortcut learning, and rejection by regulated clinical panels.

TRANSPARENT / INTUITIVE

Glass-Box Interpretable Models

Linear Models, Decision Trees & Rule Sets

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
GLASS-BOX ARCHITECTURES

Lower Accuracy / High Transparency

Transparent statistical models with direct causal interpretability.

  • Linear & Logistic Regression: Each parameter coefficient β_j represents the exact marginal rate of change in target y per unit increase in x_j.
  • Single Decision Trees: Mimics human clinical diagnostic flowcharts: "If Age > 65 and Glucose > 180 → High Risk".
  • Classification IF-THEN Rules: Disjunctive normal form rules that can be audited directly by legal compliance and clinical physicians.
  • Regulatory Gold Standard: Mandatory in high-stakes healthcare, parole sentencing, credit lending, and GDPR "Right to Explanation" domains.

THE BIAS-VARIANCE DILEMMA

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
BIAS-VARIANCE DYNAMICS

Underfitting & Overfitting

The dual failure modes of statistical generalization.

  • Underfitting (High Bias): The model is too simple to capture the underlying data manifold (e.g. fitting a straight line to a quadratic curve). Performs poorly on both train and test sets. Fix: Add features, increase model depth, reduce regularization.
  • Overfitting (High Variance): The model is overly flexible, memorizing stochastic noise and training sample idiosyncrasies. Achieves 99% train accuracy but collapses on test data. Fix: L1/L2 regularization, tree pruning, dropout, more data.

The Interpretability Imperative

Why Deploy a Model with 5% Lower Accuracy?

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
EXAMINATION QUESTION 1

Regulatory Model Selection

Under what conditions is a data scientist ethically and operationally mandated to deploy a model with lower accuracy?

  • The High-Stakes Domain Axiom: In regulated domains (credit underwriting, criminal recidivism risk, organ transplant allocation, oncology diagnosis), model decisions must be legally defensible.
  • Right to Explanation (GDPR Article 22): Consumers have a statutory right to know the exact causal factors behind an adverse automated decision.
  • Auditability & Bias Mitigation: Linear models and decision trees allow auditors to inspect feature coefficients directly to guarantee that protected attributes (race, gender, age) are not proxy-encoded.
  • Exam Conclusion: A 92% accurate decision tree is overwhelmingly preferred over a 97% accurate deep neural network when human life, civil liberty, or statutory compliance is at stake.

DIAGNOSTIC Q&A 2

DBSCAN & Anomaly Detection

Bridging Clustering and Outlier Mining

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
EXAMINATION QUESTION 2

Unsupervised Density Mechanics

How does DBSCAN uniquely bridge the boundary between unsupervised clustering and anomaly detection?

  • Density-Based Topology: Unlike K-Means, which forces every observation into one of K clusters, DBSCAN groups points based on local density (Epsilon radius ε and MinPts threshold).
  • Three Point Classifications: Points are classified as Core Points (dense clusters), Border Points (cluster edges), or Noise Points (sparse outliers).
  • Automatic Outlier Isolation: Any data point located in a low-density region that fails both Core and Border criteria is explicitly assigned cluster label -1 (Noise/Anomaly).
  • Exam Conclusion: DBSCAN simultaneously discovers arbitrary cluster shapes and extracts anomalous outliers without requiring a pre-labeled anomaly dataset.
