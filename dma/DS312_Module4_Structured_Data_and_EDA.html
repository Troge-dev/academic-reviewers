Stevens' Measurement Taxonomy: Nominal, Ordinal, Interval & Ratio

CATEGORICAL SCALES

Nominal vs. Ordinal Scales

Identity vs. Rank order

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
CATEGORICAL TAXONOMY

Nominal & Ordinal Properties

Categorical variables with qualitative distinctions.

  • Nominal (Identity): Unordered categories. Operations: Count, Mode, Equality (=, ≠). Examples: Blood Type (A/B/O), Hospital Ward, Gender.
  • Ordinal (Ranking): Ordered hierarchy without equal intervals. Operations: Median, Percentiles, Rank order (<, >). Examples: Cancer Stage (I, II, III, IV), Patient Acuity (Low/Med/High).

Interval vs. Ratio Scales

Arbitrary zero vs. Absolute zero

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
NUMERICAL TAXONOMY

Interval & Ratio Properties

Quantitative scales with measurable mathematical differences.

  • Interval (Arbitrary Zero): Equal intervals but NO absolute zero (0 does not mean total absence). Operations: Mean, +, -. Examples: Temperature in Celsius, Calendar Year.
  • Ratio (Absolute Zero): Equal intervals with meaningful absolute zero (0 means complete absence). Operations: Mean, Multiplication, Division, Ratios. Examples: Age, Length of Stay, Blood Glucose.

Outlier Detection: The 1.5 × IQR Rule & Boxplot Geometry

Quantifying statistical dispersion and calculating inner and outer anomaly fences

INTERQUARTILE RANGE
1.5×

IQR = Q3 - Q1

Lower Fence = Q1 - 1.5(IQR). Upper Fence = Q3 + 1.5(IQR). Observations beyond these fences are flagged as statistical outliers.

INNER FENCES (1.5×IQR)

Mild Outlier Boundaries

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Mild Outliers: Data points lying between 1.5×IQR and 3.0×IQR beyond the quartiles. Represent legitimate clinical extremes or measurement variance.

OUTER FENCES (3.0×IQR)

Extreme Outlier Boundaries

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Extreme Outliers: Data points beyond 3.0×IQR. High probability of data entry corruption (e.g. Length of stay = 999 days).

Donald Rubin's Missing Data Taxonomy: MCAR, MAR & MNAR

Understanding the causal probability mechanisms behind incomplete datasets

MCAR & MAR

Missing Completely at Random & MAR

Rubin's ignorable missingness mechanisms

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
IGNORABLE MECHANISMS

MCAR vs. MAR Formulations

Missingness that does not depend on the unobserved value.

  • MCAR (Missing Completely at Random): P(M|Y_obs, Y_mis) = P(M). Probability of missingness is purely stochastic. Example: Blood sample tube shatters on the floor randomly.
  • MAR (Missing at Random): P(M|Y_obs, Y_mis) = P(M|Y_obs). Missingness depends on observed variables, not the missing value. Example: Younger patients (observed age) lack blood pressure tests.
← RETURN TO SUMMARY ↻
MNAR

Missing Not at Random (Non-Ignorable)

P(M|Y_obs, Y_mis) depends on Y_mis

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
NON-IGNORABLE

Missing Not at Random Dynamics

The value itself is the direct causal driver of missingness.

  • Mechanism: The probability of missingness depends directly on the unobserved value itself.
  • Clinical Example: Severe alcoholics deliberately omit answering questions about alcohol consumption.
  • Remediation: Standard imputation causes severe bias; requires creating explicit missing indicator flags (is_missing = 1).

Statistical Testing for Missingness: Little's MCAR Test

Empirical statistical tests to diagnose whether data missingness is ignorable or non-random

LITTLE'S MCAR HYPOTHESIS TEST

H₀: Data is Missing Completely at Random (MCAR).
H₁: Data is NOT Missing Completely at Random.

If p > 0.05, fail to reject H₀ ➔ Consistent with MCAR.
If p < 0.05, reject H₀ ➔ Data is MAR or MNAR.

RODERICK J. A. LITTLE (1988) CHI-SQUARE TEST

WHOLE DATASET EVALUATION

Simultaneous Pattern Matrix Testing

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Little's Test: Compares observed variable means across all distinct missing data patterns simultaneously using a generalized likelihood-ratio chi-square.

COLUMN-BY-COLUMN TESTS

Chi-Square & T-Test Divergence

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Column Tests: Create binary missingness dummy M_j (1=Missing, 0=Observed). Run t-tests against continuous features and Chi-Square tests against categorical features.

Selecting the Correct Imputer

Decision Rule: If column tests show significant differences (p < 0.05) with other features, data is MAR ➔ Use multivariate MICE or KNN imputation.

DROPPING THRESHOLDS

Column Dropping Criteria

Standard industry thresholds for column retention.

  • > 40%–50% Missing: Drop column entirely (e.g. weight in Diabetes dataset is 97% missing).
  • < 5% Missing: Simple univariate imputation (mean/median/mode) introduces negligible bias.

Univariate statistical replacement

UNIVARIATE IMPUTATION

Mean vs. Median Rules

Replacing missing values using single-column statistics.

  • Mean Imputation: Valid ONLY for symmetric, Gaussian distributions (skewness ≈ 0). Underestimates variance!
  • Median Imputation: Preferred for skewed continuous variables or distributions with outliers.
  • Mode Imputation: Used for categorical/nominal variables.

KNN & Iterative MICE Imputation

Borrowing information across related features

MULTIVARIATE IMPUTATION

MICE & KNN Mechanics

State-of-the-art imputation preserving covariance structures.

  • KNN Imputer (k=5): Imputes values using average of the k most geometrically similar rows.
  • MICE (Chained Equations): Models each missing variable as a function of all other variables iteratively.
  • Preserves Relationships: Unlike mean imputation, MICE preserves correlations between features.

Feature Scaling Comparison: Min-Max vs. Z-Score vs. Robust

Evaluating the impact of extreme outliers and aligning feature magnitude with model requirements

BOUNDED SCALING

Min-Max Normalization

Rescaling features strictly to [0, 1]

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
MIN-MAX SCALING

x' = (x - x_min) / (x_max - x_min)

Transforms features into a fixed [0, 1] interval.

  • Formula: x' = (x - min) / (max - min).
  • Outlier Sensitivity: Extremely sensitive to outliers! A single extreme value crushes all normal data into a narrow band.
  • When to Use: Neural networks with sigmoid/softmax activations, image pixel arrays.
← RETURN TO SUMMARY ↻
VARIANCE SCALING

Z-Score Standardization

Standardizing to mean=0, std=1

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
STANDARD SCALER

z = (x - μ) / σ

Transforms features into standard normal score deviations.

  • Formula: z = (x - μ) / σ.
  • Outlier Behavior: Preserves outliers; transformed data has zero mean and unit variance.
  • When to Use: Algorithms assuming Gaussian properties: PCA, Logistic Regression, Linear Regression, SVM.
← RETURN TO SUMMARY ↻
ROBUST SCALING

Robust Scaler (Median & IQR)

Scaling using median and interquartile spread

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
ROBUST SCALER

x' = (x - median) / IQR

The premier scaling method when severe outliers exist.

  • Formula: x' = (x - Q2) / IQR.
  • Outlier Resilience: Because median and IQR ignore extremes, outliers do not skew the center or spread.
  • When to Use: Datasets with heavy tails or unverified outliers (e.g. clinical Length of Stay).

Categorical Encoding & The Dummy Variable Trap

Converting qualitative labels into binary indicator matrices while preventing perfect multicollinearity

THE DUMMY VARIABLE TRAP
k - 1

Columns Retained

When one-hot encoding a feature with k unique categories, drop 1 column in regression models. If all k columns are kept with an intercept, XᵀX is singular and non-invertible!

PERFECT MULTICOLLINEARITY

∑ x_i = 1 Collision with Intercept

Mathematical Trap: The sum of all k dummy columns equals 1.0, exactly duplicating the model intercept column. VIF explodes to infinity.

Parametric Test Assumptions: The LINE + M Framework

A systematic diagnostic framework to validate linearity, normality, homoscedasticity, and multicollinearity

LINE ASSUMPTIONS

Linearity, Independence, Normality, Equal Var

The 4 fundamental regression criteria

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
PARAMETRIC ASSUMPTIONS

The Classic LINE Assumptions

Mandatory checklist before running linear regression or ANOVA.

  • Linearity: Relationship between X and Y is linear (audited via scatter plots).
  • Independence: Observations and residuals are independent (Durbin-Watson ≈ 2.0).
  • Normality: Residuals follow Gaussian bell curve (Shapiro-Wilk test p > 0.05, Q-Q plots).
  • Equal Variance: Homoscedasticity; residual spread is constant (Levene's test p > 0.05).

+ M: MULTICOLLINEARITY

Variance Inflation Factor (VIF)

Detecting redundant collinear predictors

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
MULTICOLLINEARITY

VIF = 1 / (1 - R_j²)

Auditing correlation among independent feature columns.

  • VIF = 1.0: Completely orthogonal, zero correlation.
  • VIF > 5.0: Moderate to high multicollinearity; unstable coefficient estimates.
  • VIF > 10.0: Severe multicollinearity; mandatory to drop or merge collinear columns.

Chi-Square Test of Independence for Categorical Variables

Testing for statistically significant dependencies between two nominal or ordinal attributes

CONTINGENCY MATRIX
χ²

∑ (O - E)² / E

Compares observed cell frequencies (O) against expected frequencies (E) under the null hypothesis of independence.

NULL HYPOTHESIS H₀

Independence of Attributes

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

H₀: Variable A and Variable B are completely independent. (e.g. Insulin titration change is independent of 30-day readmission).

P-VALUE INTERPRETATION

Rejecting H₀ (p < 0.05)

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Verdict: If p < 0.05, reject H₀ ➔ There is a statistically significant association between the two categorical variables.

EXPECTED FREQUENCY RULE

Cochran's Rule: E_ij ≥ 5

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Sample Assumption: At least 80% of cells in the contingency table must have expected frequency E ≥ 5, and no cell E < 1. If violated, use Fisher's Exact Test.
