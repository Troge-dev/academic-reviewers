TOKENIZATION STRATEGIES

Word vs. Sentence Tokenization

Traditional rule-based and linguistic segmentation.

  • Word Tokenization (word_tokenize): Splits text into individual words and punctuation tokens based on punctuation boundaries and treebank rules.
  • Sentence Tokenization (sent_tokenize): Splits continuous blocks of text into individual grammatical sentences using unsupervised boundary models (Punkt).
  • Clinical Pitfall: Abbreviations with periods (e.g., "Dr.", "e.g.", "b.i.d.", "mg/dL") can cause naive regex splitters to falsely trigger sentence cuts.

MODERN SUBWORD METHODS

Byte-Pair Encoding & WordPiece

Modern statistical tokenization in clinical language models.

  • Out-Of-Vocabulary (OOV) Problem: Traditional word-level tokenizers map unseen medical jargon or rare compounds to a generic <UNK> token, losing vital diagnostic signal.
  • Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE): Iteratively merges frequent character pairs. Rare medical terms are split into recognizable morphological subwords (e.g., "hypo" + "glycemia").
  • Clinical Advantage: Enables transformers (BioBERT, ClinicalBERT) to parse novel dosage forms and medication brand names without out-of-vocabulary collapse.

Stemming vs. Lemmatization: Heuristics vs. Morphological Rigor

Contrasting crude affix-stripping algorithms with dictionary-backed Part-of-Speech canonicalization

HEURISTIC AFFIX STRIPPING

Stemming (Porter / Snowball)

Fast, Crudely Truncated Stems

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
STEMMING ANALYSIS

Porter & Snowball Mechanics

Algorithmic chopping of word endings without linguistic awareness.

  • Heuristic Affix Stripping: Applies fixed sequential transformation rules to chop suffixes like "-ing", "-ed", "-s", "-ly" (e.g., "consulting" → "consult").
  • Crucial Flaw — Non-Words: Often outputs non-dictionary strings (e.g., "hospital" → "hospit", "medical" → "medic", "stabilize" → "stabil").
  • Over-Stemming Error: Chopping disparate words to identical roots (e.g., "university" and "universe" both stemmed to "univers").
  • Under-Stemming Error: Failing to connect words with common roots (e.g., "alumnus", "alumni", "alumnae" remain separate stems).

Lemmatization (WordNet / spaCy)

Accurate Canonical Base Forms (Lemmas)

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
LEMMATIZATION ANALYSIS

WordNet & POS-Aware Lemmas

Context-aware morphological reduction utilizing lexical dictionaries.

  • Morphological & POS Awareness: Requires Part-of-Speech (POS) tagging to correctly resolve grammar (noun, verb, adjective, adverb).
  • Valid Dictionary Word Guarantee: The resulting lemma is always an actual dictionary word (e.g., "better" [adj] → "good", "saw" [verb] → "see").
  • Contextual Disambiguation: "meeting" as a noun remains "meeting"; "meeting" as a progressive verb lemmatizes to "meet".
  • Computational Trade-Off: 5× to 10× slower than rule-based stemming, but essential for biomedical precision and semantic interpretability.

Vector Space Models: Bag-of-Words (BoW) vs. TF-IDF

Contrasting naive frequency counting against statistical discriminative weighting across text corpora

RAW FREQUENCY COUNTING

Bag-of-Words (CountVectorizer)

Simple Occurrence Matrices

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
BAG-OF-WORDS ARCHITECTURE

CountVectorizer Mechanics

Vectorization based strictly on raw term recurrence in documents.

  • Unordered Token Collection: Discards word order, syntax, and grammar; represents documents solely by token occurrence counts f(t, d).
  • Extreme Sparsity: Produces massive, sparse rectangular matrices where 95%+ of cells are zero.
  • Frequency Bias Flaw: Long, verbose documents artificially dominate distance calculations due to inflated raw counts, even if content is redundant.
  • Universal Term Blindness: Words appearing in every single document receive high weights simply because of raw occurrence frequency.

TF-IDF Vectorization

Downweighting Universal Words

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
TF-IDF ARCHITECTURE

TfidfVectorizer Mechanics

Vectorization balancing document frequency against corpus rarity.

  • Dual-Weighting Balance: Rewards words frequent in a given document (Term Frequency) while penalizing words ubiquitous across the entire corpus (Inverse Document Frequency).
  • Salience Isolation: Discriminant domain words (e.g., "ketoacidosis", "metformin") receive high TF-IDF scores; ubiquitous filler words receive scores approaching zero.
  • L2 Normalization: Document length disparities are neutralized through Euclidean unit-vector normalization (||v||_2 = 1).
  • Production Baseline: The premier linear baseline for clinical classification, topic discovery, and text information retrieval.

TF-IDF Mathematical Mechanics & Step-by-Step Weighting

Formal mathematical formulation and step-by-step derivation of discriminative feature weights

MATHEMATICAL FORMULA
TF × IDF

TF-IDF(t, d, D) = TF(t, d) × IDF(t, D)

TF(t, d) = f(t, d) / ∑ f(t', d)
IDF(t, D) = ln( (1 + N) / (1 + df(t)) ) + 1

TERM FREQUENCY: TF(t, d)

Local Document Salience

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Local Importance: Ratio of the count of term t in document d to total words in d: TF(t, d) = f(t, d) / ∑ f(t', d). Measures how intensely a document focuses on that specific clinical concept.

INVERSE DOCUMENT FREQUENCY: IDF(t, D)

Global Corpus Discrimination

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Global Penalizer: Logarithm of total corpus documents N divided by document frequency df(t): IDF(t, D) = ln(N / df(t)) + 1. If a term appears in every document (df = N), its IDF approaches ln(1) = 0. Rare terms yield large positive IDF values.

SMOOTHING & NORMALIZATION

Scikit-Learn Implementation

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Smooth IDF: smooth_idf=True adds 1 to numerator and denominator inside the log: ln((1+N)/(1+df(t))) + 1. Prevents zero-division errors when a term is unseen.

UNIGRAM ISOLATION

Why Single Words Fail Context

Loss of meaning when words are examined in complete isolation.

  • Atomic Tokenization: Considers tokens individually: ["acute", "myocardial", "infarction"] or ["not", "responsive"].
  • Semantic Distortion: Cannot distinguish "high blood pressure" from "pressure" and "blood" mentioned in separate unrelated paragraphs.
  • Negation Inversion: Treats "not" and "diabetic" as independent features, losing the combined negation phrase "not diabetic".

HIGHER-ORDER COLLOCATIONS

Bigrams, Trigrams & n-grams

Capturing Phrases & Syntax

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
MULTI-WORD EXPRESSIONS

N-Gram Syntax Retention

Binding adjacent words to capture clinical idioms and negation context.

  • Bigrams (n=2): Consecutive word pairs: ("myocardial infarction", "blood pressure", "not responsive", "renal failure").
  • Trigrams (n=3): Three-word chains: ("acute kidney injury", "shortness of breath", "congestive heart failure").
  • Dimensionality Explosion: Adding bigrams/trigrams causes vocabulary size to grow exponentially (V^n), drastically increasing memory overhead.
  • Optimization Rule: Restrict vocabulary using ngram_range=(1, 2), min_df=5, and max_features=10000 to capture key collocations without memory exhaustion.

Topic Modeling: Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) Foundations

Unsupervised probabilistic generative Bayesian modeling of thematic document structures

BAYESIAN GENERATIVE MODEL

LDA assumes every document is a probability mixture over latent topics (θ_d ~ Dir(α)), and every topic is a probability distribution over words (φ_k ~ Dir(β)).

BLEI, NG, & JORDAN (2003) · LATENT DIRICHLET ALLOCATION

DOCUMENT-TOPIC DISTRIBUTION: θ_d

Dirichlet Prior α (Alpha)

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Alpha (α) Sparsity: High α means documents contain a broad mixture of many topics; low α (e.g., α < 0.1) forces documents to focus exclusively on 1–2 dominant themes.

TOPIC-WORD DISTRIBUTION: φ_k

Dirichlet Prior β (Beta / Eta)

CLICK TO FLIP ↻

Beta (β) Sparsity: High β means topics contain a diffuse distribution across most vocabulary words; low β forces topics to be defined by a concise, sharp set of specialized keywords.

Iterative Estimation: Words are iteratively reassigned to topics based on current document-topic proportions and word-topic prevalence until convergence.

LDA Model Evaluation: Coherence Score (C_v) vs. Perplexity

Resolving the statistical paradox: Why language perplexity diverges from human semantic interpretability

STATISTICAL METRIC: PERPLEXITY

Log-Likelihood Fit on Held-Out Text

Why Perplexity Fails Human Judgement

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
PERPLEXITY PARADOX

Statistical Goodness-of-Fit

Theoretical information-theoretic measure of language predictability.

  • Mathematical Definition: Perplexity measures how well the probability model predicts unseen held-out test documents: Perplexity = exp(-L / N).
  • Optimization Goal: Lower perplexity indicates superior statistical prediction of unseen tokens.
  • The Chang et al. (2009) Paradox: Human interpretability experiments proved that models with lower perplexity often produce nonsensical, uninterpretable topics ("word salad")!
  • Diagnostic Rule: Never select optimal topic count K solely based on minimizing perplexity.

SEMANTIC METRIC: COHERENCE (C_v)

Sliding-Window Word Pair Association

Human-Aligned Semantic Interpretability

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
COHERENCE METRICS

Coherence Score (C_v & U_mass)

Measures semantic similarity between high-scoring topic words.

  • Semantic Association: Calculates normalized pointwise mutual information (NPMI) and cosine similarity among top-N words of each topic.
  • C_v Coherence Range: Ranges from 0.0 to 1.0. Scores above 0.55 indicate strong thematic cohesion; scores below 0.40 indicate noisy, fragmented topics.
  • The K-Optimization Curve: Plot C_v across candidate topic numbers K in [2, 20]. Select the "elbow" or peak C_v before degradation.
  • Clinical Interpretability: Coherent topics cluster meaningful disease profiles (e.g., Topic 1: [insulin, glucose, ketoacidosis, a1c, diabetes]).

Physician Note Idiosyncrasies

Structural and lexical hurdles unique to healthcare narratives.

NEGEX ALGORITHM

Rule-Based Negation Modeling

Syntactic parsing to isolate negated clinical findings.

  • The Scope of Negation: Distinguishes confirmed diagnoses from ruled-out conditions in clinical text.
  • Pre-Condition Triggers: Phrases like "denies", "no evidence of", "patient denies", "free of" negate following concepts up to a termination boundary.

DIAGNOSTIC Q&A 1

Lemmatization vs. Stemming

Why POS Tagging is Required

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
EXAMINATION QUESTION 1

Morphological Lemmatization

Why does lemmatization require Part-of-Speech tagging while stemming does not?

  • Core Rationale: Stemming applies blind character-stripping heuristics without grammatical awareness (e.g. cutting "-ing" or "-es").
  • The POS Dependency: Words change canonical base lemmas based on their grammatical role. E.g., "running" as a verb becomes "run", but as a noun ("the running of the bulls") it remains "running".
  • Exam Conclusion: Lemmatization accesses lexical dictionaries (WordNet) indexed by POS tags; without a POS tag, the lemmatizer defaults to noun, failing on verbs like "was" → "be".

LDA Dirichlet Priors (α, β)

Impact of Small vs. Large Hyperparameters

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
EXAMINATION QUESTION 2

Dirichlet Hyperparameter Tuning

How do hyperparameter values α = 0.01 vs α = 10 affect document-topic compositions?

  • Low Alpha (α = 0.01): Induces extreme sparsity in θ_d. Each document is constrained to express only 1 or 2 dominant topics. Ideal for short texts or specialized clinical notes.
  • High Alpha (α = 10.0): Forces diffuse mixture distributions. Every document is assumed to contain a uniform blend of nearly all K topics, blurring thematic distinction.
  • Beta (β) Parallel: Low β forces topics to be defined by a few distinct words; high β causes topics to share common vocabulary.
