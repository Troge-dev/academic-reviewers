The KDD Framework: The 5 Pillars of Data Mining

Deconstructing the formal Knowledge Discovery in Databases pipeline and criteria of validity

THE 5 CRITERIA

What Makes a Pattern "Mined"?

Non-trivial, valid, novel, useful, understandable

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
KDD CRITERIA

The 5 Pillars of Pattern Discovery

Why standard SQL lookups are NOT data mining.

  • Non-Trivial: Involves inductive inference and machine learning algorithms, not simple SELECT queries.
  • Valid: Patterns must hold true on new, unseen test sets with statistical confidence.
  • Novel: Uncovers non-obvious, surprising relationships that defy superficial intuition.
  • Actionable & Understandable: Must provide clear operational utility interpretable by domain experts.

THE KDD STAGES

Knowledge Discovery Pipeline

From raw databases to business knowledge

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
KDD PIPELINE

The 5 Sequential Stages of KDD

The universal engineering framework of data mining.

  • 1. Selection: Isolating relevant target datasets from data lakes or transaction logs.
  • 2. Preprocessing: Cleaning noise, handling missing data (MCAR/MAR), and treating outliers.
  • 3. Transformation: Normalizing, scaling, encoding categorical dummies, and reducing dimensions.
  • 4. Data Mining: Applying algorithms (Random Forest, K-Means, Apriori) to discover patterns.
  • 5. Evaluation: Interpreting patterns, visualizing models, and translating insights into decisions.

MATHEMATICAL CHALLENGE

The Curse of Dimensionality

Volume grows exponentially with feature count D

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
DIMENSIONALITY TRAP

High-Dimensional Distance Collapse

Why adding more columns can destroy distance-based algorithms.

  • Hypercube Volume: As dimensions D grow, hypercube volume increases as 2^D, making data sparse.
  • Distance Metric Breakdown: The ratio of Euclidean distance between nearest and farthest points approaches 1.
  • Overfitting: Models memorize noise; mitigated via PCA, feature selection, or L1/L2 regularization.

Supervised vs. Unsupervised Learning Paradigms

Distinguishing between target-guided predictive models and pattern discovery over unlabelled manifolds

TARGET SUPERVISION

Supervised Learning

Training with ground-truth target labels y

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
SUPERVISED PARADIGM

Mapping Inputs to Target Labels

Optimizes model weights to minimize prediction error against known outcomes.

  • Data Structure: Features X paired with verified target labels y.
  • Objective: Learn mapping function f(X) ≈ y that generalizes to unseen test records.
  • Evaluation: Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, ROC-AUC, RMSE, MAE.
  • Techniques: Classification (discrete y) and Regression (continuous y).

UNSUPERVISED PARADIGM

Discovering Latent Data Structure

Explores underlying geometric distributions and co-occurrences without ground truth.

  • Data Structure: Feature matrix X with zero target labels y.

  • Evaluation: Silhouette score, Davies-Bouldin index, Support/Confidence, Explained Variance.
  • Techniques: Clustering, Association Rule Mining, PCA, Anomaly Detection.

Techniques 1 & 2: Classification vs. Regression

Contrasting discrete boundary partitioning against continuous conditional expectation surfaces

DISCRETE TARGETS

Technique 1: Classification

Predicting categorical class boundaries

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
CLASSIFICATION

Predicting Discrete Labels

Supervised task where the target variable is a discrete category.

  • Target Scale: Nominal or Ordinal classes (e.g. Readmitted: Yes/No; Disease Severity: Low/Med/High).
  • Decision Boundaries: Hyperplanes or partition trees separating feature space into class regions.
  • Key Algorithms: Decision Trees, Random Forest, XGBoost, Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machines.
← RETURN TO SUMMARY ↻
CONTINUOUS TARGETS

Technique 2: Regression

Predicting quantitative numerical trajectories

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
REGRESSION

Predicting Continuous Numbers

Supervised task where the target variable is an unbounded continuous scalar.

  • Target Scale: Ratio or Interval numbers (e.g. Length of Stay: 1 to 14 days; Patient Medical Cost: $).
  • Mathematical Objective: Minimize Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) residual sum of squares: ∑(y_i - ŷ_i)^2.
  • Key Algorithms: Multiple Linear Regression, Ridge (L2), Lasso (L1), Support Vector Regression (SVR).

Techniques 3 & 4: Clustering & Association Rule Mining

Unsupervised discovery of geometric customer cohorts and transactional market basket affinity rules

GEOMETRIC PARTITIONING

Technique 3: Clustering

Grouping unlabelled records by similarity

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
CLUSTERING

K-Means, DBSCAN & Hierarchical

Unsupervised partitioning of data points based on geometric proximity.

  • K-Means: Partitions data into k clusters minimizing Within-Cluster Sum of Squares (WCSS).
  • DBSCAN: Density-based spatial clustering capable of discovering arbitrary non-spherical shapes.
  • Evaluation: Silhouette Coefficient (range -1 to +1; score > 0.5 indicates dense, well-separated clusters).

MARKET BASKET MINING

Technique 4: Association Rules

Discovering transactional affinities: A ➔ B

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
ASSOCIATION RULES

Support, Confidence & Lift

Unsupervised discovery of item co-occurrence rules in transactional logs.

  • Support: P(A ∩ B) = Frequency of joint occurrence across all transactions.
  • Confidence: P(B|A) = Conditional certainty of purchasing B given A.
  • Lift: P(A ∩ B) / (P(A)·P(B)). Lift > 1.0 indicates strong positive correlation over random chance.

OUTLIER PROFILING

Technique 5: Anomaly Detection

Flagging rare deviations from normal distributions

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
ANOMALY DETECTION

Isolation Forests & Local Outlier Factor

Identifying fraudulent transactions, equipment failures, or clinical anomalies.

  • Isolation Forest: Recursively isolates observations; anomalies require fewer random splits to isolate.
  • Local Outlier Factor (LOF): Measures local density deviation relative to surrounding k-nearest neighbors.
  • Challenge: Severe class imbalance (anomalies represent < 0.1% of transactions).

VARIANCE PRESERVATION

Technique 8: Dimensionality Reduction

Compressing feature dimensions while preserving variance

CLICK OR TAB TO FLIP ↻
DIMENSIONALITY REDUCTION

Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

Defeating the Curse of Dimensionality through orthogonal linear projections.

  • PCA Mathematics: Computes eigenvectors and eigenvalues of the feature covariance matrix.
  • Orthogonal Components: Principal components are completely uncorrelated (VIF = 1.0).
  • Elbow Rule: Retain components explaining 80%–90% of total cumulative variance.

Executive Checkpoint: Technique Selection Rubric

DECISION RULE 1

Supervised vs. Unsupervised

DECISION MATRIX

Target Variable Verification

Determine your primary learning paradigm in seconds.

  • Have y labels? Supervised. If y is discrete (Readmitted) ➔ Classification. If y is continuous (Stay Length) ➔ Regression.
  • No y labels? Unsupervised. If grouping rows ➔ Clustering. If finding item affinities ➔ Association Rules. If compressing columns ➔ PCA.

Association Rule Evaluation

Support vs Confidence vs Lift Decision

ASSOCIATION RULES

Interpreting Market Basket Metrics

Defending association rule validity.

  • Support: Filters out rare coincidences (minimum threshold e.g. 0.05).

  • Lift > 1.0: True statistical association; eliminates rules driven purely by ubiquitous popular items.
